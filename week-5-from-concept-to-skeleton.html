Week 5

From concept to grey box

Component 1 goes in on Friday. A peer show of concepts and studio time on the document, then your storyboard becomes a grey-box page that fixes the pacing, and the first thing moves.

The pre-production ends here. What you hand in this week is what you spend the next eight weeks building.

That is worth taking literally. Component 1 is not an essay about a piece you might make; the concept you submit becomes the narrative you build, and your response to the feedback becomes the first evidence of iteration in Component 2. There is no fresh start in Week 7.

So the first half of today is the document: a peer show, then time to fix what it tells you. The second half puts the storyboard into the browser as a page with nothing in it but the pacing, and makes the first thing move. Both are kept light, because Friday is close.

The deadline

Component 1 (Research & Concept, 30%) is uploaded to MyBeckett by Friday 30 October at 12 noon. Today is a working session on a submission that is days away, not weeks.

From the assignment brief

What Component 1 asks for

One PDF, uploaded to MyBeckett by Friday 30 October, 12 noon, with your AI-use log as a separate file. Six things, in this order. All six are on the table today.

  1. Story research: your verified story or topic, with credible sources showing what is true and how you know
  2. Key influences: the interactive narratives and other work that have shaped your thinking, and what you are taking from each
  3. Audience and intent: who the piece is for and what you want them to understand or feel
  4. Your concept: premise and experience arc. What does your reader encounter, understand and do from the top of the page to the end?
  5. Moodboard and concept sketches: the visual direction of the piece (imagery, type, colour, tone) and early sketches of how it might look
  6. Interaction intent: a motion storyboard or annotated sketch of the scroll sections showing pacing beats, key transitions and at least one designed moment of genuine interaction

Four marking criteria, 25% each: research and influences; audience and intent; concept and visual direction; interaction intent. The wording above is the brief's own. If this page and the brief on MyBeckett ever disagree, the brief wins.

Part one

Six items, four criteria

The six items above are marked on four criteria, each worth a quarter of the component. That is the single most useful fact on this page: every year somebody submits fifteen pages of research and half a page of storyboard, and wonders where the marks went.

Which items feed which criterion. Each criterion is 25% of Component 1.
Criterion Fed by The top band wants
Research the story and its influences Story research; key influences Every claim verified against credible sources, research that visibly shapes the piece, and influences you can say something specific about.
Define the audience and intent Audience and intent Audience precisely defined; intent clear enough that it drives every later design decision.
Develop a concept and visual direction for a scroll-driven narrative Your concept; moodboard and concept sketches A concept that could only work as a scrolling narrative, and a visual direction you could build from.
Communicate interaction intent through a motion storyboard Interaction intent Pacing beats annotated, every decision justified against the story, at least one designed moment of choice.

Two phrases to design your document around

“Research that shapes the piece.” Somewhere in your research section, say what you found that changed your plan. If your source list has the “what this changed about my piece” line from the research methods tutorial, this writes itself.

“Every decision justified against the story.” Annotations that say what happens are worth a pass. Annotations that say why this movement serves this moment are worth a first.

One PDF. Make the storyboard legible at full width rather than four frames to a page, and check it opens on a machine that is not yours before noon on Friday.

Part two

The peer show

In fours. Everyone shows, everyone responds. This is not a critique of how good your work is; it is a test of whether your concept survives contact with someone who is not you.

Peer show · about 35 minutes · groups of four

Three minutes each, then two of response

  1. Three minutes. Your pitch sentence, then walk your storyboard. Do not apologise for anything and do not explain what it will look like when it is finished.
  2. Everyone else writes first, silently. One minute. Writing before speaking stops the loudest person in the group setting the verdict.
  3. Two minutes of response, using the four prompts below, in order. The person showing does not defend, argue or explain. They write.
  4. Rotate. Four people, about twenty-five minutes.

The four prompts: use these words

  • What I understood the piece was about. One sentence, in your own words.
  • What surprised me: the bit I did not already know.
  • Where I got lost: the first place the order stopped making sense.
  • What I wanted to touch: where my hand went, whether or not anything is there.

The first prompt is the important one. If three people describe your piece three different ways, your concept is not yet a concept. That is a fixable problem on a Tuesday and an expensive one in January.

The fourth is the one people underrate. Where a reader's hand goes is where the interaction wants to be, and it is very often somewhere you had not planned.

Studio time · about 40 minutes · on your own document

Fix what the peer show told you

Straight afterwards, before you talk yourself out of any of it: write down the three things you are going to change, then work on the document. I go round the room. The order to work in, if you are not sure:

  1. The cheap fixes first. Reordering two beats takes five minutes on a beat sheet and an hour on a storyboard. Do it on the sheet, then redraw only the frames that moved.
  2. The thinnest of the six items next. Look at the list at the top of this page and find the heading with the least under it. That is where the next twenty minutes go, not the section you enjoy.
  3. The interaction moment. Drawn and justified, not mentioned. If somebody's hand went somewhere in the peer show, that is a data point: write it in.
  4. The credits. Every image in the document that you did not make has a source next to it. Do it now while you remember where they came from.

Keep the notes

The three things you decided to change, and why, are the first entry in your iteration trail. Component 2 asks for exactly that, and it starts here.

Break

Part three

The grey box

Last week you wrote a scroll-height estimate beside every beat. Now you find out how wrong you were, in the cheapest way there is: a page with one plain section per beat, each the height you guessed, and nothing in it but a label. Scroll it. Adjust the heights until the pacing feels right. That page is the grey box, and the brief asks for it by name: an early, unstyled version of your page that fixed the scroll distances, kept in your GitHub history.

It is also the skeleton your build hangs off from Week 7. The markup is deliberately dull. One section per beat; where a scene holds several beats, one section with the held picture and the steps you built in Week 2.

index.html

<section class="chapter" id="beat-01-cover">
  <h2>Cover</h2>
</section>

<section class="chapter" id="beat-02-then-and-now">
  <h2>Then and now</h2>
</section>

<section class="scene" id="beat-04-decisions">
  <div class="scene-sticky">
    <img src="images/scene-02.svg" alt="What the map shows.">
  </div>
  <div class="scene-steps">
    <div class="step"><p>Beat 4: the first decision.</p></div>
    <div class="step"><p>Beat 5: the second.</p></div>
    <div class="step"><p>Beat 6: the third.</p></div>
  </div>
</section>

And the CSS that makes it a pacing test, under YOUR STYLES:

css/style.css

/* Grey box: one screen per beat until proven otherwise */
.chapter {
  min-height: 100svh;          /* one screen. Change per section below. */
  border-bottom: 1px solid var(--ink);
}

#beat-02-then-and-now { min-height: 250svh; }   /* a scrub: two and a half screens */

Three things are doing real work there. The id on each section is numbered, then named, so the reading order is visible at a glance and the name tells you which beat it is; next week your triggers aim at those ids. The min-height in svh is the pacing, in the unit you storyboarded in. And the border is only there so you can see where one beat ends when there is nothing else on the page.

Keep the class names you already have

chapter, chapter-figure, chapter-text, scene, scene-sticky, scene-steps and step all came from your starter and Week 2, and every recipe from here on assumes them. Renaming them is a silent way to make every example on the module site stop working for you. Match your Figma frame names to your section ids, so a comment in a crit points at one thing.

Task 1 · 25 minutes · on your own project

Build your grey box

  1. Open your project. Keep the cover; you can delete the Week 2 practice chapter and scene, or leave them and add below.
  2. One <section> per beat, in order, top to bottom, with a real heading in each: the heading from your beat sheet, not “Section 3”. Where a scene holds several beats, one .scene with one .step per beat.
  3. Give every section the height you estimated last week, in svh. Save. Scroll the whole thing, top to bottom, at reading speed.
  4. Adjust. One beat drags, another rushes. Change the number, save, scroll again. Keep going until the page feels like your storyboard reads. Write the final numbers back on the storyboard.
  5. Put a comment where your interaction moment goes: <!-- INTERACTION: before/after slider -->
  6. Drag the window down to phone width. The heights are in screen units, so nothing should break; if something does, it is the Week 2 scene and the fix is on the Week 2 page.
  7. Commit with the message grey box, exactly that, and sync. This commit is the one the brief asks for in your GitHub history; you will point at it in your evaluation.

Part four

First motion

Four weeks with no animation, on purpose. You have a story, a beat sheet and a page that paces it, so now the library gets to serve them. Today is the smallest possible contact: link it, make one thing arrive as the reader reaches it, watch it work, commit. The full toolkit starts in Week 7, when Component 1 is behind you.

The library is GSAP, with its ScrollTrigger plugin. It is what most of the pieces on your watch-list are built with, it is free, and it is two script tags away.

index.html, just above your own script tag

<script src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/gsap@3.15.0/dist/gsap.min.js"></script>
<script src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/gsap@3.15.0/dist/ScrollTrigger.min.js"></script>
<script src="js/main.js"></script>

Then, in main.js, four things: tell GSAP about the plugin, ask whether this reader wants motion, and if they do, make every chapter heading rise into place as it arrives.

js/main.js

gsap.registerPlugin(ScrollTrigger);

var mm = gsap.matchMedia();

mm.add("(prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)", function () {

  gsap.utils.toArray(".chapter h2").forEach(function (heading) {
    gsap.from(heading, {
      opacity: 0,
      y: 40,                      // starts 40px lower than its resting place
      duration: 0.8,
      ease: "power2.out",         // arrives fast, settles gently
      scrollTrigger: {
        trigger: heading,
        start: "top 80%",         // fires when the heading is 80% down the screen
        markers: true             // DELETE BEFORE YOU PUBLISH
      }
    });
  });

});

Two lines are worth understanding today; the rest can wait for Week 7.

  • gsap.from() means start from these values and end wherever the CSS already puts it. If this script never runs, the heading is simply there. That is why nothing in your CSS ever says opacity: 0: the page must read perfectly with no JavaScript at all, and from is what keeps that true.

  • gsap.matchMedia() asks the reader's device a question before doing anything. Some people have asked their phone or laptop for less motion, for reasons ranging from vestibular disorders to preference, and their setting is called reduced motion. Everything animated on this module goes inside that block, from this first line onwards, so those readers get the page exactly as it is without the script: complete, readable, still. That is not a lesser version. It is the version you just built.

Task 2 · 25 minutes · on your own project

Make one thing move

  1. Add the two script tags above your own. Save, reload, open the console: no red. If there is red, the order of the tags is wrong or a URL is mistyped.
  2. Type the main.js block. Type it, do not paste it; you are going to write a lot of these.
  3. Scroll. Green and red lines appear at the right-hand edge: those are the markers, showing where each trigger fires. Watch a heading rise as its start line meets the scroller-start line.
  4. Change "top 80%" to "top 50%". Scroll again. Notice the headings now arrive after you have started reading them, which looks broken. Put it back.
  5. Change y: 40 to y: 400, then to y: 0. Decide which you like. Most people land somewhere under 60.
  6. Turn reduced motion on and reload: on a Mac, System Settings, Accessibility, Display, Reduce motion; on Windows, Settings, Accessibility, Visual effects, Animation effects. Nothing moves, and the page is complete. Turn it back off.
  7. Set markers: false. Commit with the message first motion, and sync. Check the live address.

Then stop

Do not add a second effect today. Component 1 is due on Friday and none of it is code. The library will still be there in Week 7.

Part five

Submitting

Before Friday, 12 noon

  • All six items present, under their own headings, and roughly in proportion to the four criteria.
  • Storyboard legible at full size, with the notation key on it, and a why-line under each frame.
  • Your interaction moment drawn and justified, not just mentioned.
  • Sources listed properly, with dates checked.
  • Credits complete for every image you did not make.
  • AI-use log uploaded as its own file, even if it says you used none.
  • Uploaded through the MyBeckett link for Component 1, then downloaded again and opened, to prove it arrived intact.
  • Today's grey box and first motion commits synced to GitHub.

That last item is process evidence rather than the submission itself, but the brief is explicit that work you cannot evidence or explain does not earn marks. The commits are what make your authorship visible, and they only work if they have been accumulating.

If something goes wrong on Friday morning, upload what you have before noon and email me. A slightly incomplete submission on time is an entirely different situation from a late one.

If you get stuck

My storyboard is not finished and the deadline is Friday

Finish it roughly rather than perfecting half. Twelve annotated frames at medium quality mark better than five beautiful ones and a gap, because the criterion is about communicating the whole piece. Frames for beats you are unsure about can say so: a note reading “undecided between these two” is honest design thinking, not a hole.

The peer show made me want to change everything

Normal, and usually an overreaction. Change the order and the opening if they need it; both are cheap. Do not change your topic four days before the hand-in. Write the bigger doubts down and bring them to the Week 7 studio; some of them will turn out to be the iteration evidence Component 2 wants.

How long should the document be?

There is no word count. As a rough shape: a page or two of research with sources, half a page of influences, half a page on audience and intent, a page on the concept, a page of moodboard and sketches, and then the storyboard, which is usually the bulk of it. If your storyboard is not the longest part, the balance is probably wrong.

Can I still change my mind about the interaction moment?

Yes, and many people will. Component 1 asks you to have designed one, not to be bound to it for ever. Changing it later with a reason is iteration; never having designed one is a missing criterion.

My grey box looks terrible

It should. It is a pacing test, not a design. Judge it on whether it reads top to bottom in the right order with the right headings and whether the scroll feels like your storyboard. That is the only question today. Styling starts once the structure has stopped moving.

Nothing moves

In order: is there red in the console? Are the two GSAP script tags above main.js? Does your page have any .chapter h2 elements for the loop to find? Is reduced motion switched on at system level? And are you looking at the Live Server tab rather than a file you double-clicked?
