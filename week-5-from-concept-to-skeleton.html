Week 5

From concept to skeleton

Component 1 goes in this week. In the studio: a peer show of concepts, then your storyboard becomes real HTML sections, so the build starts with something already standing.

The pre-production ends here. What you hand in this week is what you spend the next eight weeks building.

That is worth taking literally. Component 1 is not an essay about a piece you might make; the concept you submit becomes the narrative you build, and the feedback you get back in Care Week becomes the first evidence of iteration in Component 2. There is no fresh start in Week 7.

Which is why we spend the second half of today writing HTML. A storyboard that turns into markup in the same week it is finished is a storyboard you trust. One that sits in Figma until Week 7 tends to quietly stop matching what you build.

The deadline

Component 1 (Research & Concept, 30%) is uploaded to MyBeckett by Friday 30 October at 12 noon. This session falls inside that week, so today is a working session on a submission that is days away, not weeks.

Part one

What you are submitting, exactly

Four things. They are weighted equally, at 25% of the component each, and that is the single most useful fact on this page — every year somebody submits fifteen pages of research and half a page of storyboard, and wonders where the marks went.

Component 1 deliverables against the marking criteria. Each is worth a quarter.
You submit It is marked as The top band wants
Story research — your verified story with credible sources Research and verify the story Every claim verified against authoritative sources, and research that demonstrably shapes the narrative arc.
Audience and intent — who it is for, what you want them to understand or feel Define the audience and intent Audience precisely defined; intent articulated clearly enough that it drives every later design decision.
Your concept — premise and experience arc Develop a concept suited to scroll-driven narrative A concept that could only work as a scrolling narrative, with moments of genuine agency already identified.
Interaction intent — the motion storyboard Communicate interaction intent through a motion storyboard Pacing beats annotated, every decision justified against the story, at least one designed moment of choice.

Two phrases to design your document around

“Demonstrably shapes the narrative arc.” Somewhere in your research section, say what you found that changed your plan. If your sources.md has the “what this changed about my piece” line from the research methods tutorial, this writes itself.

“Every decision justified against the story.” Annotations that say what happens are worth a pass. Annotations that say why this movement serves this moment are worth a first.

Format is up to you — a PDF is the safe default and travels best through MyBeckett. Make the storyboard legible at full width rather than four frames to a page, and check it opens on a machine that is not yours before noon on Friday.

Part two

The peer show

In fours. Everyone shows, everyone responds. This is not a critique of how good your work is; it is a test of whether your concept survives contact with someone who is not you.

Peer show · about 45 minutes · groups of four

Three minutes each, then two of response

  1. Three minutes. Your pitch sentence, then walk your storyboard. Do not apologise for anything and do not explain what it will look like when it is finished.
  2. Everyone else writes first, silently. One minute. Writing before speaking stops the loudest person in the group setting the verdict.
  3. Two minutes of response, using the four prompts below, in order. The person showing does not defend, argue or explain. They write.
  4. Rotate. Four people, twenty minutes, two rounds if there is time.

The four prompts — use these words

  • What I understood the piece was about. One sentence, in your own words.
  • What surprised me — the bit I did not already know.
  • Where I got lost — the first place the order stopped making sense.
  • What I wanted to touch — where my hand went, whether or not anything is there.

The first prompt is the important one. If three people describe your piece three different ways, your concept is not yet a concept. That is a fixable problem on a Tuesday and an expensive one in January.

The fourth is the one people underrate. Where a reader's hand goes is where the interaction wants to be, and it is very often somewhere you had not planned.

Then twenty minutes of quiet

Straight afterwards, before you talk yourself out of any of it: write down the three things you are going to change, and make the cheap ones now. Reordering two sections takes five minutes on a beat sheet. Keep the notes — responding to feedback is evidence of iteration, and Component 2 asks for exactly that.

Break · 15 minutes

Part three · Task

From storyboard to skeleton

Your storyboard is a stack of sections. So is your HTML. The translation is close to mechanical, and doing it today means Week 7 opens with your own real page rather than a blank file.

No JavaScript today, and no animation. What you are building is the readable baseline the brief requires — the version that still tells the story with scripts disabled. Get that right and the rest of the module is layering. Get it wrong and you will be fighting your own markup in Week 8.

A plain section

Most of your piece is this, repeated.

index.html

<section class="chapter chapter--figure"
         id="chapter-02-then-and-now"
         aria-labelledby="chapter-02-then-and-now-heading">

  <div class="chapter__text">
    <h2 id="chapter-02-then-and-now-heading">Then and now</h2>
    <p>One idea. Two or three paragraphs at most.</p>
  </div>

  <figure class="chapter__figure">
    <img src="images/1972.jpg" alt="What the picture shows, not that
         it is a picture">
    <figcaption>What it is, and who it is by.</figcaption>
  </figure>
</section>

Three things are doing real work there. The <section> with a heading is what makes the page navigable to a screen reader. The id is what your ScrollTrigger will aim at in Week 7. And the alt text is a marked accessibility requirement that takes ten seconds now and is tedious to retrofit across forty images.

A scene

Two or three of these in the whole piece. A held visual, with steps that scroll over it — the structure you drew last week, and the one already sitting in your starter at #scene-1. The sticky block and the steps occupy the same grid cell, and that overlay is what makes the text pass across the picture rather than sit beside it.

index.html

<section class="scene" id="scene-04-decisions"
         aria-labelledby="scene-04-decisions-heading">

  <h2 id="scene-04-decisions-heading">How it happened</h2>

  <div class="scene__sticky">        <!-- the bit that holds still -->
    <figure class="scene__figure">
      <img src="images/map.jpg" alt="...">
      <figcaption class="scene__caption">First state.</figcaption>
    </figure>
  </div>

  <div class="scene__steps">
    <div class="step" data-step="1"><p>First thing that happened.</p></div>
    <div class="step" data-step="2"><p>Then this.</p></div>
    <div class="step" data-step="3"><p>And then this.</p></div>
  </div>
</section>

Why data-step

Your starter already carries data-step on each step, numbered from 1. In Week 2 the watcher used its own loop counter instead; from Week 7 we read the attribute, so you can reorder your steps in the HTML without renumbering anything. You will do that more than once.

Task · 45 minutes · on your own project

Build your section skeleton

  1. Open your project from Week 2. Work on a copy if you would rather not disturb it.
  2. One <section> per row of your beat sheet, in order, top to bottom.
  3. Real headings, in order, no levels skipped. Placeholder body text is fine for now — but the headings should be the real ones.
  4. Mark your two or three scenes with the scene structure above. Do not style them yet.
  5. Drop in whatever images you have. Low-resolution placeholders are fine; write the real alt text anyway.
  6. Put a comment where your interaction moment goes: <!-- INTERACTION: before/after slider -->
  7. Read the whole thing top to bottom in the browser with no CSS applied at all. It should still make sense. That is the no-JavaScript baseline the brief asks for, and it is easiest to check now.
  8. Commit and sync. Check your live URL.

There is a worked example

The section skeleton on the module site is a complete, commented example of the structure above — twelve sections including two scenes and a marked interaction point. Read it, take what is useful, do not hand it in.

Part four

Naming things that survive to Week 8

Small discipline, disproportionate payoff. In three weeks you will be writing selectors against this markup at half past eleven at night.

  • Number your section IDs, then name them. chapter-02-then-and-now, not section2 or aral. The number gives you reading order at a glance; the words tell you which one it is. Your starter uses chapter-2 and scene-1 — same prefix, same -heading suffix on the heading ID, with a slug added because twelve sections need one.
  • Match your Figma frame names to your section IDs. Same names in both places, so a comment in a crit points at one thing.
  • Keep the class names you already have. chapter, chapter--figure, chapter__text, chapter__figure, scene, scene__sticky, scene__steps and step all came from the starter, and every recipe from Week 7 onwards assumes them. Renaming them is a silent way to make every example on the module site stop working for you.
  • One idea per section, still. If a section has two headings in it, it is two sections.
  • Do not style for animation yet. No opacity: 0 anywhere. That is the Week 2 rule and it is what keeps the no-JavaScript baseline readable.

Part five

Submitting

Before Friday, 12 noon

  • All four parts present, and roughly balanced — they are worth the same.
  • Storyboard legible at full size, with the notation key on it.
  • Your interaction moment drawn and justified, not just mentioned.
  • Sources listed properly, with dates checked.
  • Image credits complete for anything you did not make.
  • AI-use log uploaded as its own file, even if it says you used none.
  • Uploaded through the MyBeckett link for Component 1 — then downloaded again and opened, to prove it arrived intact.
  • Figma file shared with me, version history visible.
  • Today's skeleton committed and synced.

That last pair are process evidence rather than the submission itself, but the brief is explicit that work you cannot evidence or explain does not earn marks. The commits and the Figma history are what make your authorship visible, and they only work if they have been accumulating.

If something goes wrong on Friday morning, upload what you have before noon and email me. A slightly incomplete submission on time is an entirely different situation from a late one.

Week 6

Care Week

No taught session next week. Three things happen:

  • Component 1 feedback comes back during the week, in time to shape the build rather than after it.
  • An optional drop-in for anyone who wants it.
  • A recorded Rive and Spline demo goes on MyBeckett. Awareness only — watch it if you are curious about where this work goes next. Nothing assessed depends on it.

Week 7 opens with ScrollTrigger proper, applied to the skeleton you built today. Come back with your feedback read and your sections in order. There is no directed task over Care Week beyond that; it is called Care Week for a reason.

If you get stuck

My storyboard is not finished and the deadline is Friday

Finish it roughly rather than perfecting half. Twelve annotated frames at medium quality mark better than five beautiful ones and a gap, because the criterion is about communicating the whole piece. Frames for sections you are unsure about can say so — a note reading “undecided between these two” is honest design thinking, not a hole.

The peer show made me want to change everything

Normal, and usually an overreaction. Change the order and the opening if they need it — both are cheap. Do not change your topic four days before the hand-in. Write the bigger doubts down and bring them to the Week 7 studio; some of them will turn out to be the iteration evidence Component 2 wants.

How long should the document be?

There is no word count. As a rough shape: a page or two of research with sources, half a page on audience and intent, a page on the concept, and then the storyboard, which is usually the bulk of it. If your storyboard is not the longest part, the balance is probably wrong.

Can I still change my mind about the interaction moment?

Yes, and many people will. Component 1 asks you to have designed one, not to be bound to it for ever. Changing it later with a reason is iteration; never having designed one is a missing criterion.

My skeleton looks terrible

It should. It is unstyled markup. Judge it on whether it reads top to bottom in the right order with the right headings — that is the only question today. Styling starts once the structure has stopped moving.
