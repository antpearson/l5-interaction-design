Interaction Design · Weeks 2 and 3

Ideas kit

Print this before Week 2. The first three pages are the Week 2 ideas activity and your directed task; the rest is the Week 3 topic workshop. Laptops stay shut for both, so you will want somewhere to write.

Week 2, round one: change over time

Something that changed, or a story that unfolded. One line for each prompt that sparks something; skip the rest. At least six, from either list.

From your own life

  • A building near you that used to be something else.
  • A shop, café or venue you used to go to that has closed or changed.
  • A field, wood or patch of land near you that has been built on.
  • A park, river, beach or green space near you that has changed.
  • Something about where you grew up that's different from when you were small.
  • A job that a machine or an app now does.
  • A music, gaming or online scene you're part of that has changed since you got into it.
  • An app, website or piece of tech everyone used that has disappeared.
  • Something you buy that costs a lot more than it used to.

From the wider world

  • A person, famous or local, with a life story worth telling.
  • An expedition, voyage or journey that you find interesting.
  • A race, rivalry or record attempt.
  • A disaster, rescue or escape.
  • A mystery that was never fully solved.
  • A moment in history you'd like to see from the inside.
  • A town, coastline or landscape that looks completely different from a hundred years ago.
  • A glacier, forest or river that has shrunk or grown.
  • An animal, plant or habitat that is disappearing, or coming back.
  • An invention that changed how people live.
  • A band, brand, club or company that rose and fell.

Week 2, round two: explainers

How something works, or how to do it. Same again: at least six.

From your own life

  • A hobby or skill that you'd like to educate people on.
  • Something you know more about than anyone else in this room.
  • Something about a job you've done that customers never see.
  • A place you've seen behind the scenes: a kitchen, warehouse, stage or stadium.
  • The rules of a sport or game that newcomers might find confusing.
  • An interesting dish you cook that involves a number of steps.
  • Something you collect that most people wouldn't know about.

From the wider world

  • Something you've always wondered how it works.
  • Something you use every day without knowing how it works, like a contactless card or a fridge.
  • How an instrument or piece of kit makes its sound, like an electric guitar pickup.
  • What a production role actually involves, like a cinematographer.
  • A practical skill done properly, like sharpening a knife.
  • How something gets made, from raw material to shop shelf.
  • How something is built: a bridge, a ship, a stage set.
  • A place you'd like to walk someone through: a cave, a ship, a castle, a mine.
  • How a big system works behind the scenes: the power grid, the post, a football transfer.
  • How a meme or video takes off and spreads.
  • Why something happens: tides, the northern lights, a traffic jam with no obvious cause.
  • How an animal or plant does something remarkable.

Then swap sheets and add one idea to three of someone else's answers.

The five tests: your directed task before Week 3

Run your five starred ideas through these at home and write the answers down. Arrive in Week 3 with the three that passed at least four. One fail is fixable. Two, bring it to the pitch. Three, it is a different topic.

  1. Is there a change? Something is different at the bottom of the page from the top: in the world, or in the reader's head.
  2. Can you see it? Find five images or a dataset you are allowed to use, before you commit.
  3. Can you check it? Every claim needs a source you could show me. This is a quarter of Component 1.
  4. Is there a moment where the reader does something? Point at a place where letting them act would mean something.
  5. Do you care? Eleven more weeks, in evenings, when it is not working.

The four shapes

Change over timeDown is later. “What happened to it?”
Linear revealDown is the next thing you are allowed to know. “What happens next?”
Spatial journeyDown is further along or deeper in. “Where am I now?”
Layered explainerDown is one more layer on the same object. “How does it work?”

All four have one thing in common: down means something. If your sections could be shuffled without loss, the scroll is decoration.

The pitch

“My piece is about [what], for [who], so that they understand or feel [what]. It works as a scroll because [which shape, and why down means something]. My evidence so far is [sources]. Somewhere around [roughly where], I think the reader should get to [do something].”

Then one line on what worries you about it. That line is the most useful thing you will say.
