Round B — prompts

Three answers each, minimum. First thing, second thing, then the stupid thing.

  1. Something that used to be everywhere and now is not.
  2. A building near me that has had at least three different lives.
  3. A number that has gone one direction for fifty years.
  4. Something everyone thinks they understand and almost nobody does.
  5. A job that has nearly disappeared in my lifetime.
  6. Something my grandparents would not recognise about where I grew up.
  7. A thing I have to explain every time it comes up.
  8. Somewhere I can walk to that has a story attached.
  9. Something that got better, quietly, and nobody made a fuss.
  10. A decision that gets made for people without them noticing.

Then swap sheets and add one idea to three of someone else's answers.

Round C — what you already know

Five things you know more about than the average person in this room. Then, for each, the one sentence a stranger needs to hear.

The five tests

Run every candidate through these. One fail is fixable. Two, come and talk to me. Three, it is a different topic.

  1. Is there a change? Something is different at the bottom of the page from the top — in the world, or in the reader's head.
  2. Can you see it? Find five images or a dataset you are allowed to use, before you commit.
  3. Can you check it? Every claim needs a source you could show me. This is a quarter of Component 1.
  4. Is there a moment where the reader does something? Point at a place where letting them act would mean something.
  5. Do you care? Eleven more weeks, in evenings, when it is not working.

The four shapes

Change over timeDown is later. “What happened to it?”
Linear revealDown is the next thing you are allowed to know. “What happens next?”
Spatial journeyDown is further along or deeper in. “Where am I now?”
Layered explainerDown is one more layer on the same object. “How does it work?”

All four have one thing in common: down means something. If your sections could be shuffled without loss, the scroll is decoration.

The pitch

“My piece is about [what], for [who], so that they understand or feel [what]. It works as a scroll because [which shape, and why down means something]. My evidence so far is [sources]. Somewhere around [roughly where], I think the reader should get to [do something].”

Then one line on what worries you about it. That line is the most useful thing you will say.
